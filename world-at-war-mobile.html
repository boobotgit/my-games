Articles of War

— how the campaign is fought —

The Board

The world is divided into 42 land territories across six continents and 19 sea zones across three naval theatres. Land holds armies; sea holds fleets. All land begins claimed. All seas begin unclaimed and empty — the navies of the world are yet to sail.

1 · Muster

  • Armies = territories ÷ 3 (minimum 3) + continent bonuses. Place on any land you hold.
  • Fleets = coastal territories ÷ 3 (minimum 3) + seas ÷ 3, rounded down — the same rule as armies. Place them in a sea you already hold, or in any empty unclaimed sea touching your coastline — which claims it outright.
  • Holding an entire continent or naval theatre pays a bonus every turn.

2 · Campaign

Attack from any region holding 2 or more units. Attacker rolls up to 3 dice (one fewer than its units, max 3); defender rolls up to 2. Highest against highest; ties favour the defender. You choose how many dice to roll: more dice win battles faster, but when you take a territory you must march in at least as many armies as dice you rolled in the final round. Drop to 1 die for the last push if you want to keep your army at home.

  • Army → adjacent land — an ordinary land assault.
  • Army → adjacent sea — a flotilla is launched; victorious armies become fleets. An empty sea needs no battle — any coastal territory with 2+ armies can simply sail into it and claim it. This is the fastest way to build a navy.
  • Fleet → adjacent sea — a naval battle. An empty unclaimed sea is simply occupied.
  • Fleet → adjacent land: BOMBARDMENT. Ships cannot take ground. A bombardment kills defending armies but can never reduce a territory below 1, and captures nothing. Use it to soften a coast before you land.
  • Amphibious assault. Two land territories with no shared border may still fight — but only if you command a sea zone touching both. This is the sole road between continents: Britain, the Americas, Japan and the Indies fall only to a power that rules the waves.

3 · Manoeuvre

Once per turn, move any number of units between two regions you hold, so long as an unbroken chain of your own regions connects them. Units crossing from land to sea become fleets, and fleets coming ashore become armies.

Dispatch Cards

Take at least one region off a rival in a turn and you draw a card. Sailing into an empty unclaimed sea is not a conquest and earns nothing. Three of a kind — or three different — may be exchanged at muster for a swelling reward (4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, then +5 each). Five cards in hand forces an exchange. If a card in the set shows ground you already hold, 2 extra armies muster there at once. Break a power utterly and its cards pass to you.

Your Rivals

Every rival power is dealt one of five doctrines at the start of the campaign, and it shapes everything they do — how slim the odds they will accept, whether they stack one fist or guard every border, how hard they fight for the sea, and when they break off a losing assault. The doctrine is named under each power in the list on the right; hover it for what it means.

  • Conqueror — attacks on the slimmest odds and rarely breaks off.
  • Sentinel — digs in, strikes only at certainties, hoards its cards.
  • Admiral — rules the waves first and shells the coast before landing.
  • Opportunist — hunts the weakest border it can find and finishes cripples.
  • Strategist — plays for whole continents and the bonuses they pay.

Ruin

A power is finished the moment its last land territory falls. Ships take no ground, so a navy without a homeland has no road back — when the flag comes down its fleets strike their colours, and every sea it held falls open to be claimed afresh. Sinking an enemy battle fleet is one way to break a rival; taking its last coast is the surer one.
